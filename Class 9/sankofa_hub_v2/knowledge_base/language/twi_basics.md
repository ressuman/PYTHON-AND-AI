# Twi Language — Basics & Reference Guide

## Overview of Twi
Twi (pronounced "chwee") is the most widely spoken language in Ghana, used by approximately 9 million speakers as a first language and millions more as a second language for inter-ethnic communication. It belongs to the Kwa subgroup of the Niger-Congo language family. Twi is one of the Akan languages, which also include Fante, Akuapem Twi, and Asante Twi. The two most prominent dialects are Asante Twi (spoken in the Ashanti Region, centred on Kumasi) and Akuapem Twi (from the Eastern Region, historically used in Bible translation and literacy materials). Asante Twi is the more widely spoken variety in everyday urban life.

## Twi is a Tonal Language
Twi uses tone to distinguish meaning — the same syllable spoken on a high tone vs. a low tone can mean entirely different things. There are two primary tones: High (H) and Low (L). For example:
- "papa" (H-H) = good/fine
- "papa" (L-L) = father
- "papa" (H-L) = a type of fan
Tones are rarely marked in everyday writing but are critical for spoken accuracy. Non-native speakers should be patient with themselves — tone acquisition takes time.

## Pronunciation Notes
Vowels in Twi are pure (not diphthongised as in English):
- a = as in "father"
- e = as in "bed"
- ɛ = as in "bed" (slightly more open)
- i = as in "machine"
- o = as in "go"
- ɔ = as in "law"
- u = as in "boot"

The letter 'ky' represents a sound similar to 'ch' in English "church" — e.g., "kyɛ" (give).
The letter 'hy' represents a sound similar to 'sh' — e.g., "hyɛn" (ship).
'dw' sounds like a combination of 'd' and 'w' — e.g., "dwom" (song).

## Cultural Context in Greetings
Greetings are of immense cultural importance in Twi-speaking communities. Skipping a greeting when entering a room or meeting someone is considered rude. The standard greeting sequence involves asking about health, family, work, and the household. Rushing through or skipping any part signals disrespect. Elders are always greeted first. When entering a group, greet each person individually if the group is small enough.
