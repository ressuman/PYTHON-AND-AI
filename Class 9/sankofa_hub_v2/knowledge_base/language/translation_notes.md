# Translation Notes — Twi, Ga, Ewe & Common Phrases

## Translation Quality Disclaimer
Machine translation for Twi, Ga, and other Ghanaian languages is significantly less reliable than for major European languages. AI models are trained on far less data for these languages. When translation accuracy is important (legal documents, medical communication, formal ceremonies), always use a human interpreter or translator. The translations below are provided as best-effort reference material, not authoritative translations.

## Twi Translations — Common Requests

### Family Terms
| English | Twi | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mother | Maame / Ɛna | Maame is the most common |
| Father | Agya / Papa | Papa is common in southern usage |
| Elder sibling | Onua (general term for sibling) | Twi uses same term for brother/sister |
| Child | Onipa ketewa / Abofra | Abofra specifically means child |
| Grandmother | Nana (also an honorific for respected elders) | Context determines meaning |
| Grandfather | Nana | Same term — context/gender of person clarifies |

### Proverbs (Ɔbεn)
Twi proverbs (ɔbεn) are central to Akan communication, especially in formal and elder speech. They convey wisdom indirectly.

- "Onipa na ɔhyε onipa ɛkwan mu" — "It is people who pave each other's way" (we need each other)
- "Ɛyɛ asɛm a, na wo werɛ nfiri" — "If it is a matter, do not forget" (accountability)
- "Obra yɛ w'adwuma a, na wo ho yɛ" — "If you treat life as work, your situation improves" (diligence)
- "Obi kyere obi akyi, obi nso kyere obi anim" — "Someone points someone's back; someone else points their face" (perspective depends on position)

## Ewe Translations — Basic Reference
Ewe is spoken in the Volta Region of Ghana and across the border in Togo. Approximately 3 million speakers in Ghana.

| English | Ewe |
|---|---|
| Hello | Ŋdi (morning) / Ŋdɔ (afternoon) / Fiɛ gbɔna (evening) |
| How are you? | Aleke nèle? |
| I am fine | Ele nyuie |
| Thank you | Akpe |
| Yes | Ɛ̃ |
| No | Ao |
| Goodbye | Héyi nyuie (go well) |

## AI Translation Guidance
When using this system (or any AI) for translation:
1. For casual conversation learning: reasonably reliable for common phrases.
2. For written materials: use as a draft only; verify with a native speaker.
3. For formal/legal/medical use: do not use AI translation. Use a certified human translator.
4. Tonal languages: AI text translation cannot capture tone — actual pronunciation requires hearing from a native speaker or a verified audio resource.
