# Travel Safety in Ghana

## General Safety Assessment
Ghana is widely regarded as one of the safest countries in West Africa for
international visitors. It has maintained political stability since 1992 and
has a strong democratic tradition — peaceful transfers of power between
opposing parties have occurred multiple times. Violent crime specifically
targeting tourists is relatively rare. The main safety concerns for visitors
are petty theft, road traffic accidents, and health risks — not political violence.

## Petty Theft
Pickpocketing and opportunistic bag snatching occur in crowded areas of Accra
and other major cities. Areas requiring extra vigilance:
- Makola Market and Kejetia Market (Kumasi) — very crowded, easy for thieves
- Tro-tro and bus stations — crowded, distracted travellers are targets
- Beaches, particularly Labadi — unattended bags on beach get taken
- Street markets and any large public gathering

Precautions:
- Keep phones and cameras inside a bag or pocket when not in use
- Use Bolt/Uber rather than walking through unfamiliar neighbourhoods at night
- Do not display expensive jewellery, cameras, or phones unnecessarily
- Use a money belt for passport and large amounts of cash
- Keep your hotel's address written down separately from your phone

## Road Safety
Road traffic accidents are statistically the most significant safety risk
for visitors in Ghana — more so than crime.

Key facts:
- Ghana's roads outside major cities are frequently unmarked, poorly lit,
  and in variable condition
- Many commercial vehicles (tro-tros, trucks) are poorly maintained
- Night driving outside Accra and Kumasi is not recommended
- Driving under the influence is common — be cautious on weekend nights
- Pedestrians and livestock regularly walk on main highways

Recommendations:
- Use Bolt or Uber in cities rather than unmetered taxis where possible
- For intercity travel, choose STC or VIP Bus over tro-tros for safety
- If hiring a car, use a driver familiar with local roads
- Always wear a seatbelt — insist on it even if others in the vehicle do not
- Allow extra journey time — rushing leads to risk-taking

## Health Precautions

### Malaria
Malaria is prevalent throughout Ghana year-round in all regions.
It is a serious risk — do not underestimate it.
- Start antimalarial medication before travel (consult your doctor 4–6 weeks ahead)
- Use DEET-based insect repellent, especially during the evening
- Sleep under a mosquito net if your accommodation is not fully sealed
- Wear long sleeves and trousers after dusk
- Seek medical attention immediately if you develop fever, chills, or headache
  during or after your visit

### Yellow Fever
A valid yellow fever vaccination certificate is mandatory for entry into Ghana.
Enforced at the border and airport. Vaccination must be administered at least
10 days before travel to be considered valid.

### Water
Tap water in Ghana is not safe to drink. Use bottled water or treated water
for drinking and brushing teeth. Bottled water is widely available and cheap.

### Medical Facilities
International-standard medical facilities exist in Accra:
- Trust Hospital (private, Accra) — recommended for visitors
- Korle-Bu Teaching Hospital — largest public hospital, basic standards
- Nyaho Medical Centre (private, Accra) — good for non-emergency care

Outside Accra, medical facilities are more limited. For serious illness or
injury, medical evacuation to Accra is sometimes necessary.
Travel health insurance with emergency evacuation coverage is strongly recommended.

## Northern Ghana Border Areas
The northern border regions (Upper West, Upper East) adjacent to Burkina Faso
and northern Côte d'Ivoire have seen occasional security incidents related to
regional instability that extends from the Sahel. This does NOT affect the
main tourist destination of Mole National Park, which is considered safe.
However, check your government's current travel advisory before visiting
communities very close to the northern borders.
The Greater Accra, Central, Ashanti, Eastern, and Volta regions — where the
vast majority of tourism takes place — have no elevated security concerns.

## Common Tourist Scams to Avoid
**The "friendly local" guide** — someone approaches you near a tourist site,
insists on showing you around "for free," then demands large payment or leads
you to a shop where they earn commission.
Fix: Arrange guided tours through your hotel or official tourist board offices.

**Unofficial currency exchange** — someone offers a better exchange rate than
the bank. Always use banks or licensed forex bureaux. Unofficial exchangers
often short-change or use sleight of hand.

**"Duty-free" sellers before the official zone** — sellers approach you
before you reach the actual duty-free section at KIA.
Fix: Walk past and use the official duty-free shops only.

**Fake police checkpoints** — rare but reported on some routes to the north.
Legitimate police checkpoints are official and uniformed. If in doubt, ask
to call the Tourist Police number above.
