# Transport in Ghana

## Getting to Ghana
Kotoka International Airport (KIA, IATA: ACC) in Accra is the main international gateway.
Airlines serving KIA include: British Airways and Ghana Airways (London), KLM (Amsterdam),
Brussels Airlines (Brussels), Emirates (Dubai), Delta and United (New York/Atlanta),
Ethiopian Airlines (Addis Ababa), Kenya Airways (Nairobi), and many African regional carriers.
Always book return tickets in advance — last-minute fares on popular routes can be very expensive.

## Getting Around Ghana

### Domestic Air Travel
Domestic flights connect Accra, Kumasi, Tamale, and Takoradi.
Africa World Airlines and Passion Air are the two main domestic carriers.
- Accra to Kumasi: ~45 minutes by air vs. 4–5 hours by road
- Accra to Tamale: ~1 hour by air vs. 9–11 hours by road
Book in advance — flights fill up, especially on Fridays and Sundays.
Last-minute fares are significantly higher.

### Intercity Road Travel (Buses)
STC (State Transport Company) operates air-conditioned coaches between major cities.
Considered the most reliable and comfortable bus option. Fares are affordable.
- Accra to Kumasi: approximately GHS 60–80, journey time 4–5 hours
- Accra to Cape Coast: approximately GHS 35–50, journey time 2.5–3 hours
- Accra to Tamale: approximately GHS 120–160, journey time 9–11 hours

VIP Bus is another quality intercity operator with similar standards to STC.
Buses depart from their dedicated terminals — confirm the terminal location before travel
as STC and VIP use different departure points in Accra.

Local tro-tros (minibuses) are the cheapest intercity option but depart only when full,
have no fixed timetable, are often overcrowded, and are statistically less safe.
Recommended only for budget travellers comfortable with uncertainty.

### Taxis and Ride-Hailing in Cities
Metered taxis are scarce in Ghana — always agree on a fare BEFORE entering a taxi.
Failure to agree first almost always leads to overcharging disputes on arrival.

Bolt and Uber both operate in Accra and Kumasi. Strongly recommended for visitors:
fares are fixed and metered, drivers are registered and accountable, and you have a
record of the journey. Accra airport to Osu: approximately GHS 80–120 via Bolt.

Okada (motorcycle taxis) are common in smaller towns and as a last-mile option.
Helmets are not always provided — use with personal judgement on safety.

### Car Rental
International companies (Hertz, Sixt, Europcar) operate at Kotoka Airport and in Accra.
A local driver is strongly recommended for visitors unfamiliar with Ghanaian roads —
road conditions, unmarked hazards, and local driving norms require experience to navigate safely.
Driving is on the RIGHT in Ghana. An international driving permit is required alongside
your home country licence.

## Traffic in Accra
Accra traffic congestion is significant, particularly on the N1 motorway (Accra–Tema road),
the Ring Road, and routes into and out of the city centre.
- Morning rush: 7:00–9:30am
- Evening rush: 4:00–8:00pm
Allow at least 60–90 minutes extra for airport transfers during rush hours.
The Accra Metropolitan Area is expanding rapidly — traffic patterns change frequently.
Local drivers and Bolt/Uber drivers will know current best routes.

## Road Safety Notes
Road traffic accidents are one of the most significant safety risks for visitors in Ghana.
Key precautions:
- Always wear a seatbelt — enforcement is increasing but inconsistency exists
- Avoid night driving outside city areas — roads are often unlit and unmarked
- Pedestrians frequently walk on highways — drive with extra caution
- Livestock on roads is common in rural and peri-urban areas
- Road surfaces deteriorate significantly during rainy season (April–October)
  particularly on secondary and tertiary roads
