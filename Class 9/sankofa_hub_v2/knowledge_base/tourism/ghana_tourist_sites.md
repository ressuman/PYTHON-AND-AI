# Ghana Tourist Sites & Attractions

## Cape Coast Castle & Slave Dungeons
Cape Coast Castle is one of the most significant historical sites in Ghana and West Africa. Built by the Swedish in 1653 and later controlled by the British, it served as a major hub in the transatlantic slave trade. The castle houses the infamous "Door of No Return" through which enslaved Africans passed before being shipped to the Americas. Today it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a deeply moving memorial. Visiting hours are 9am–5pm daily. Entry fee is approximately GHS 100 for international visitors and GHS 20 for Ghanaian citizens. Guided tours are included in the entry fee and last approximately 1 hour. Located in Cape Coast, Central Region. The Ghana Museums and Monuments Board manages the site.

## Kakum National Park
Kakum National Park, adjacent to Cape Coast, is a tropical rainforest covering approximately 375 square kilometres. Its famous canopy walkway stretches 330 metres at a height of 30–40 metres above the forest floor, offering views of the forest canopy and resident wildlife including forest elephants, bongo antelopes, and over 600 butterfly species. The walkway accommodates a maximum of 6 visitors at a time due to conservation and safety requirements. Opening hours: 8am–4pm daily. Entry fees: GHS 80 (international adults), GHS 40 (international children), GHS 20 (Ghanaian adults). The park is best visited early morning for bird-watching. Nearby: Hans Cottage Botel offers accommodation with crocodile ponds.

## Mole National Park
Mole National Park is Ghana's largest wildlife sanctuary, covering 4,840 square kilometres in the Northern Region. It protects over 90 mammal species including African elephants, buffalo, roan antelope, waterbuck, green monkey, and kob. Walking safaris with armed rangers depart twice daily (6:30am and 3:30pm) from the Mole Motel, the park's main accommodation. Jeep safaris are also available. Entry fees: approximately USD 10 for foreign nationals per day. The park is accessible by road from Tamale (approximately 3–4 hours) or by charter flight. Best time to visit: November to April (dry season), when animals congregate at water holes and vegetation is lower. The Mole Motel has a viewing platform overlooking a watering hole frequented by elephants.

## Labadi Beach (La Pleasure Beach)
Labadi Beach is Accra's most popular public beach, located in the La suburb of Accra about 10km east of the city centre. Entry fee: approximately GHS 30–50. Facilities include beach bars, restaurants, jet ski rentals, horse riding, and live music on weekends. The beach is busiest on weekends and public holidays. Safety note: ocean currents along Ghana's coast can be strong; swim only in designated safe areas and heed lifeguard warnings. Nearby: Kokrobite Beach (30km west of Accra) is a quieter, more bohemian alternative popular with surfers and musicians.

## Elmina Castle
Elmina Castle, built by the Portuguese in 1482, is the oldest European building in sub-Saharan Africa. It is located in Elmina, 10km west of Cape Coast. Like Cape Coast Castle, it was central to the transatlantic slave trade and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Joint tickets covering both Elmina and Cape Coast Castle are available at a discount. A lively fish market operates directly below the castle walls. The Dutch Reformed Church inside the castle grounds is a remarkable contrast to the dungeon beneath it.

## Wli Waterfalls
Wli Waterfalls, near Hohoe in the Volta Region, is the highest waterfall in West Africa, with the upper falls dropping approximately 143 metres. The lower falls are accessible via a 45-minute guided hike through forest and across a stream. Swimming is permitted at the base of the lower falls. The upper falls require a more strenuous 3–4 hour return hike. Entry fee: approximately GHS 50. Best visited during the rainy season (April–October) when water flow is strongest. Fruit bats roost in vast numbers on the cliff faces near the lower falls.

## Aburi Botanical Gardens
The Aburi Botanical Gardens, established by the British in 1890, cover 64 hectares in the Eastern Region, approximately 35km north of Accra. Home to over 600 plant species including rare tropical hardwoods, flowering plants, and an avocado grove. Popular for picnics and family outings. Opening hours: 8am–6pm daily. Entry: GHS 20. The nearby Aburi town is known for its furniture craftspeople and cooler highland climate.
